# Flyer study (Shary, 2026-11-15)

Shary shared 20 event, conference and promo flyers. These notes record why they work. Shoof copies only the principles and generic colour schemes, never the designs themselves.

## Colour
- **One plus one plus one.** Each flyer uses one dominant colour, one neutral (white, cream, charcoal or black) and one opposite accent:
  - navy or blue + orange (business seminar, "5 step" conference)
  - teal + orange (tech event)
  - navy + yellow (yoga)
  - purple + white (business conference)
  - magenta + navy (music)
  - cyan + amber on light grey (infographic)
- **Where the accent goes.** It lands on the things to read first: the date, the price, the button, and one shape.
- **Photos are tinted.** A colour overlay or duotone makes the photo part of the palette and keeps white text on top readable (festival, tech round, fundraising).
- **Luxury stays quiet.** It uses one deep colour (maroon) on stone or cream, with thin caps and lots of space (Insignia).
- **Loud styles stay controlled.** Colour-block and splash designs use a black or charcoal base, so many bright colours still look controlled.

## Layout
- **Two zones.** There is a bold zone (colour band, tinted photo or dark panel) and a calm zone (white or light) for the details.
- **The zones meet at an angle or curve.** The join is a diagonal, a curve, an angled wedge or a wave, which gives movement.
- **The headline has strong contrast.** It combines a huge heavy, often condensed word with a lighter or script second word.
- **Facts sit in tags.** The date, time and price go in ribbons, pills or circle badges that overlap the edge between the zones.
- **People sit in repeated shapes.** Speakers are shown in circles or hexagons with an accent ring and a name label.
- **The accent repeats.** The same accent appears about three times (the tag, a decorative shape, the button), so the eye travels down the page.
- **Details stay structured.** Thin rules, small-caps labels and icon rows (music, food, place) keep the details organised.
